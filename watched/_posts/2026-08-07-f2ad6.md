Besides being visually stunning, I always find adaptations interesting because I like seeing how someone translates a story from one medium to another. 

Some of my favorite parts:
<!--more-->
- One that I found surprisingly resonating with me is when Odysseus is finally ready to leave Calypso, and she tells him:

> You're a man who needs to control his fate. But you cannot control this. You must give yourself to the storm, give yourself to Poseidon. And take your punishment. Give up the fight, give up control and live. It is a leap of faith you haven't yet made, Odysseus.

(thanks [IMDB](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt33764258/quotes/?item=qt8879188&ref_=ext_shr_lnk))

For someone who finds leaps of faith challenging, this hit close to home.

- Everyone is complaining (and rightfully so) about the things Nolan left out, but I'm just happy he _didn't_ leave out the story of [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argos_(dog)) 🥲 (the only other cinematic adaptation I've seen, the one from 1997 with Arman Assante, did not include it)

Interesting things people are saying elsewhere:

- [This post by @claireheywoodauthor on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbVnODeDQH5/) makes a very interesting point on why Nolan's Odysseus lacks some of the more complicated (even problematic) characteristics present in the Homeric poem.
- [This other post by @kellysarri on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbciR8hiB-u/) argues that maybe the best thing about the movie isn't the movie itself, but the effect it is having on people, i.e. the renewed interest in reading and discussing classical texts and in the humanities in general.
