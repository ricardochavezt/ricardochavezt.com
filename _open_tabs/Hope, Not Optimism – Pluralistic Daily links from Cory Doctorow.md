As always, excellent insights from Cory Doctorow on how to remain hopeful nowadays (and always) and why is it different from being optimistic.

The main points:

- To face problems in the real world, too complex and large to fully optimize, we should do like the computer scientists and make peace with that fact, opting for a "good enough" solution instead of a perfect one.
- One good approach (the one favored by Doctorow at least) for social problems is [hill climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_climbing): take one step at a time towards highest ground

> If you can see just one way — any way — to improve your situation, no matter how seemingly small, then you will ascend a step up the hill, you will find a new vantage point, and new courses of action will be revealed. Take note of these, determine which one gets you the furthest toward your goal, take another step, and reassess.

- [Lawrence Lessig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Lessig) argues in his book [_Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace_](https://lessig.org/product/code) that there are 4 forces that determine social outcomes:
	- **Code:** That which is technically possible or impossible.
	- **Law:** That which is legally permitted or prohibited.
	- **Markets**: That which is profitable or unprofitable.
	- **Norms:** That which is socially acceptable or unacceptable.

- For Doctorow, those are the 4 cardinal directions when climbing the metaphorical hill:
> This suggests a method: If you feel hopeless and out of options, stop to check whether you can switch directions and still climb the gradient.
- This method "allows each individual or collective or affinity group to pick a tactic that moves them up the hill toward their goal, with the understanding that allies with different priorities, skills, and vantage points can leverage their successes for more success."
> The less time we all have to spend agreeing on tactics and timing, the more time we have to actually advance those tactics.

- This method is not optimistic, it is _hopeful_:
> Optimism is just a form of fatalism, a view of the world in which our deeds are irrelevant to the outcome: “No matter what I do, things will get better.”
>

> 
> Hope is a _method:_ If I do something about this situation, I might change it enough so that I can do something else about this situation.
